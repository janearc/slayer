# Sources for the Humboldt squid

Each entry says what the source supports and what it does not. The sizes
are description ranges for the species, not measurements of the modelled
individual. No photograph is imported as an asset.

- FAO, Cephalopods of the World, Dosidicus gigas entry, pp. 301-302
  (fao.org/4/i1920e/i1920e07.pdf), and its Fig. 293 on printed p. 301. The
  mantle is robust and thick-walled. The broad rhomboid fins have a reported
  width of 49 to 65 per cent and a length of 41 to 49 per cent of mantle
  length. Adult arm tips are elongated with many small suckers, and the
  tentacular clubs are distinct.
- NOAA, Northeast Pacific Invertebrates, species entry
  (repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/56171/noaa_56171_DS1.pdf), printed
  p. 104. Its dorsal and sucker-detail photographs give the silhouette,
  fins, arm orientation and sucker scale. The photograph shows a landed
  animal, not a swimming pose.
- Smithsonian, Larval and Juvenile Cephalopods, species account and Fig. 172
  (repository.si.edu, SCtZ-0513). It describes species features and
  growth-stage illustrations. Juvenile chromatophore patterns are not adult
  surface markings.
- Field study of the locomotion and behaviour of Humboldt squid, 2012
  (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22915711/). Observed climb-and-glide motion is an
  active ascent followed by a fins-first downward glide. It is a behavioural
  reference, and the authored loop does not replicate it.
- Aperture effects in squid jet propulsion, 2014
  (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24501132/). It concerns paralarvae of about 1 mm,
  and its kinematics do not transfer to adults.

## What the sources do not give

Eight arms and two separate feeding tentacles are required. A muscular
hydrostat has no vertebrate spine, so a flexible internal pen does not justify
a vertebrate body rig as an anatomical claim. Sucker counts and fine
structures depend on resolution. Adult chromatophore and photophore sources
were not read closely enough to support a material model, so colour is
authored and no bioluminescence is claimed.
