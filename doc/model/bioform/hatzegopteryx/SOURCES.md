# Sources for the Hatzegopteryx studies

Each entry says what the source supports and what it does not. None of
them measures a whole Hatzegopteryx: the holotype is fragmentary, and
every dimension, colour and motion in the studies is chosen.

## Specimens

- Holotype FGGUB R1083: rear skull material and an incomplete humerus.
  Buffetaut and others (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12061403/) extrapolated a
  wingspan of at least 12 m and a skull nearly 3 m. Those are estimates.
  The studies use an 11 m span as an authored intermediate scale for a
  giant azhdarchid.
- EME 315: a robust posterior cervical vertebra referred to Hatzegopteryx
  sp., not part of the holotype. Naish and Witton (2017,
  pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5248582/) estimate a relatively short,
  robust neck, depending on the vertebra's position, and decline to infer a
  wingspan from one vertebra. The studies use a neck of about 1.5 m as an
  illustrative control length, derived from the model and not measured.

## Related animals

- Zhejiangopterus, the primary skeletal figure in Witton and Naish (2008),
  PLoS ONE, doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0002271 (figure 1, g001): the
  skeleton and wing plan are extrapolated from it. It is a related
  azhdarchid, not Hatzegopteryx.

  The paper describes the extent of
  azhdarchid wing membrane as uncertain, and long toothless rostra and long
  hindlimbs as group traits, with fragmentary Hatzegopteryx fossils and
  differences between genera.
- Elgin and others, Acta Palaeontologica Polonica 56
  (app.pan.pl, app20090145.pdf): membrane impressions of azhdarchoid
  pterosaurs. No Hatzegopteryx membrane outline is known, so the wing edge
  and the hindlimb attachment are choices.
- Kellner and others (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2842671/), section
  3(c): in the non-azhdarchid Jeholopterus, specimen IVPP V12705, several
  fibre layers run in different directions. A distal region has visible
  actinofibrils and a proximal region has none. The authors say the
  composition, and so the mechanical properties, are unresolved, and read
  tension control in flight as plausible, not observed.

  Any anisotropy,
  pretension or stiffness used for Hatzegopteryx is an authored hypothesis.
- The Quetzalcoatlus functional study (doi.org/10.1080/02724634.2020.1780247)
  compares skeletal joint ranges and conditions its quadrupedal launch
  reconstruction on unmeasured forces and tissue properties. It is an
  analogy, not measured Hatzegopteryx motion.

## Engineering analogy

- Menzel and others, the JWST sunshield design paper
  (ntrs.nasa.gov, 20230009106): membrane edge catenaries and compliant
  borders. The manufactory's Webb shield draws static boundary geometry with
  authored sine-scalloped edges; it solves no tension. The studies took the
  idea of an explicit boundary with exact anchor points and nothing more.
  Living tissue is not Kapton.
