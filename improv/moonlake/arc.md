# moonlake's arc

2026-11-10, early morning, as it was said, then mapped to the song's
measured grid (score.yaml; bar 1 beat 1 is 59.09 s, verified by eye).
"it's a light show. it's not realistic. it's to be beautiful." "the moon
is a character."

The view, from the first ask (2026-11-09): "looking down at the water
through trees." The camera up in the spruces, looking down at the lake
through branches; the far rim and the sky above.

## in her words

> i want the scene to start *dark*. i want the moon to rise, into the
> scene, reflecting on the water, but i want it to be very faint and dim
> as the music progresses from quiet and pensive to full energy, and
> then i want it to suddenly ILLUMINATE and kind of shock the viewer,
> and then go dark, have the lights go, and then come back.

> when the break hits everything going dark, and then the harsh overhead
> light, illuminating everything with a very unusual color and
> intensity

and earlier: "incredibly bright light from above, like a spaceship, cool
blue, not quite tungsten, but BRIGHT AS DAYLIGHT, and then fade back to
the 'natural' lighting of the moon and the mist and trees and lasers".

> moon becomes fully illuminated and present in the scene at [1:38.9].
> yes. and there's another crucial point at ~2:19. it should rapidly
> fade to dark, and then BOOM the overhead and then the acid comes back
> at 2:46, and the moon becomes *another light in the frame* and the
> whole scene is lit, with the moon pulsing, and the overhead crashing
> on the big hits,

> and then it calms down aroun 3:00, all the lights are on, but the
> overhead isn't so hard, the moon is static -- this happens in the
> club, the lights come up and you can see what's going on around you
> but the strobes and lasers are still going on, it's .... kind of
> grounding?

> then the moon starts to set back the way it came and dims around
> 3:13, and the lights dim again slowly, lasers and strobes off or
> gentle,

> and then at 3:39 (there's a bunch of fast hits right before that,
> where the lasers should be going crazy) you have to have a huge fire
> explode on the scene and send up sparks, *every single downbeat* and
> then at 3:52 or so, the overhead comes on bright and harsh for just a
> moment, and then everything fades to black.
