# heard.

Screenplay v3 - 15 November 2026

A wordless music film. We search the sky, find something larger than we
can understand, and discover that it has heard us.

This replaces the previous working screenplay and cue allocation. It is
written from the edited screenplay-v2.txt and the later notes.
The rejected movies remain historical takes. These are new shot intentions,
not descriptions of footage already rendered.

The first arrangement follows this script: 72 bars, 4/4, 66 BPM, followed
by its complete release tail. Score time is 4:21.818 before that tail.
Glass Hour's motifs and dark, wide night-pad character remain the musical
basis. Breaks and impacts are rewritten for the action below. These are
specific editorial choices for review, not previously approved timings.

## The film

### 01. Listening

Bars 1-2; 0:00.000-0:07.273.

Darkness. A narrow spectrogram advances through it. A second display listens
to the same sound at another frequency scale. Bright ridges emerge from the
dark; we have time to notice that something is being examined. At bar 2 beat
3, heard. appears, small, lowercase, with its period. The traces recede before
the first landscape.

Music: Keep Glass Hour's opening figure recognisable. Low bass and a thin
delayed lead; no percussion announcement. The display follows the actual
finished mix, including its quiet gaps.

### 02. Two listeners

Bars 3-4; 0:07.273-0:14.545.

Both Kecks stand above the wooded Cloudsea slope, separated by warm layers of
mist. Near-black branches and a broken rock anchor the foreground; a service
light catches equipment beside the fixed foundations. With the camera almost
still, both instruments begin the same slew and settle together. Their paired
movement is the first unmistakable action.

Music: Carry the original brittle motif through the cut. One low accent starts
the pair; the second bar gives their movement room to finish.

### 03. Somewhere else

Bars 5-5; 0:14.545-0:18.182.

Rubin appears against a bare Chilean ridge. Its faceted enclosure and long
service wing interrupt the rounded Hawaiian silhouettes. The opening follows
the instrument; the building below stays grounded. We arrive while it is
already working.

Music: A short metallic tick changes the texture. No crash and no matching
four-laser rig.

### 04. Under Subaru

Bars 6-7; 0:18.182-0:25.455.

Inside Subaru, near the floor. Warm light runs across the mirror and pale
trusses. The instrument moves above the stable floor and enclosure ribs. The
camera eases upward through the aperture toward the field it has chosen. Let
the instrument settle before the camera finishes.

Music: The lead leaves gaps around a slower pad. The sustained harmony carries
the upward view without an accent on every movement.

### 05. The large listener

Bars 8-9; 0:25.455-0:32.727.

Inside ELT, the primary's edge crosses the foreground and layered structure
rises overhead. We can follow a launcher from its mounting inside the
enclosure into a sharp amber beam passing through the aperture. A structural
member hides part of the beam as the optics change elevation. Hold the scale.

Music: A low struck tone, followed by space. Remove the original early snare
wash; this introduction should not spend the film's climax.

### 06. What they are looking at

Bars 10-11; 0:32.727-0:40.000.

From behind both Kecks, the camera tilts into a Milky Way that fills the sky.
The pair abandons one field, slews to a different elevation and settles
together. Stars and the Milky Way turn on one sky clock. Mist continues
independently beneath them.

Music: The questioning lead returns with a few dry ticks. The camera reveals
the sky across the phrase, without a zoom on each one.

### 07. A city still awake

Bars 12-12; 0:40.000-0:43.636.

Low through Los Angeles haze, Griffith Observatory sits above scattered city
lights. Give it one held, human-scale view. This is another place looking
outward, not a replacement for a principal instrument.

Music: Thin the music to a low bass and a small unanswered lead phrase. Let
the scale briefly feel familiar.

### 08. Sunset

Bars 13-13; 0:43.636-0:47.273.

The Sun itself descends behind the shoulder of a Chilean instrument and
crosses the ridge in frame. Hold the silhouette as the last rim disappears.
The source of light and the shadows agree.

Music: A falling phrase loses its percussion. The image earns the transition;
there is no generic exposure fade.

### 09. Dawn without relief

Bars 14-14; 0:47.273-0:50.909.

A later Hawaiian morning. Both Kecks remain in silhouette while the Sun
emerges behind the terrain and lights the top of the cloud sea. A short copper
rim is all the warmth the sky offers. Cut into a later night rather than
racing the Moon through another arc.

Music: One dark sustained interval. No rising major chord and no celebratory
cymbal.

### 10. An unfinished search

Bars 15-16; 0:50.909-0:58.182.

Back inside Rubin, a mirror changes elevation and holds. Cut to Subaru's rear:
its open slit frames a different field. At the next phrase the Kecks reject
their current target together. Each cut advances the work; none repeats the
earlier camera arc. Roads, retaining walls, trees and equipment give the
instruments a place to stand.

Music: The lead becomes more insistent and short ticks accumulate. This is the
first real build. Reserve the larger drum sounds.

### 11. The last decision

Bars 17-17; 0:58.182-1:01.818.

Both Kecks begin a final small correction. Match its direction across a cut to
Rubin, then ELT. Different machines finish different motions. By the last beat
each has nearly settled. The camera slows with them.

Music: The moving figure stops after beat 2. No new notes on beats 3-4; allow
the room and delayed notes to drain into the lock.

### 12. They have found something

Bars 18-19; 1:01.818-1:09.091.

At 18.1 the Kecks stop together. Cut once to the Chilean instruments already
holding the authored common direction. Then stay. Mist moves; the search does
not. The frame offers no object to explain their attention. A very slow
approach begins only after we recognise the stillness.

Music: Two bars without new notes in any role. This is a withdrawal with
audible decay, not a promise of digital silence. No crash at the lock.

### 13. The first radio field

Bars 20-21; 1:09.091-1:16.364.

A compact array fills a wide shot. The dishes turn together above fixed piers,
finish their adjustment and hold. Keep enough separate antennas in frame to
make their agreement visible.

Music: A single low onset starts the field. A sparse two-note figure follows;
no marching beat beneath every dish.

### 14. The second radio field

Bars 22-23; 1:16.364-1:23.636.

A more widely spread array answers from a different angle. The aim agrees with
the first field in the story. After the dishes settle, return briefly to an
optical beam that has remained steady. Hold the last beat before leaving
Earth.

Music: Another low onset, then a shortened phrase. Bar 23 stops after beat 2,
leaving a gap before the first answer.

### 15. First answer: the orbital

Bars 24-25; 1:23.636-1:30.909.

A restrained impact opens space. Reveal enough of the orbital structure to
understand its orientation before anything wakes. An immense dark curve
catches scattered light, with another structure behind it. Hold long enough to
find our bearings.

Music: The first crash is a low hit with a metallic wash, marked mf. Bass
drops a register. The answering lead enters after the impact, not on top of
every visual detail.

### 16. Zappy speaks

Bars 26-27; 1:30.909-1:38.182.

An electrical pulse crosses a readable part of the machinery. Follow its route
past fixed joints; a second, shorter pulse changes direction. The structure
remains intelligible when the light recedes. End on a held dark shape, not a
flare.

Music: A precise, short electrical phrase with displaced ticks. Leave the
final two beats of bar 27 without new notes so the lantern can answer.

### 17. The lantern

Bars 28-31; 1:38.182-1:52.727.

A warmer object answers with a slow swell across its panels. Light gathers,
passes through openings, then subsides. We learn its complete shape and one
patient gesture. Briefly return to Zappy listening in darkness before the
lantern finishes.

Music: Long pad notes replace the busy figure. A soft bell and a slower
rising-and-falling lead give the lantern its own voice. Keep it uneasy despite
the warmer colour.

### 18. The small fold

Bars 32-35; 1:52.727-2:07.273.

In one readable composition, a dark rib turns edge-on in bar 32. In 33 the
opening widens while its surrounding frame contracts. In 34 a lit junction
passes behind another and emerges in a relation that should not fit. In 35 the
structure settles. Keep three identifiable junctions visible throughout. This
exact four-stage gesture must be remembered.

Music: A new four-bar motif: C-sharp, F-sharp, G, then an unresolved low
F-sharp. One metallic punctuation accompanies the crossing; the settled bar is
mostly room. Save this motif for the final reveal.

### 19. The shaped absence

Bars 36-39; 2:07.273-2:21.818.

A luminous field loses a precise piece of itself. The occulter crosses it
slowly; a bright edge vanishes and another returns somewhere unexpected. We
recognise a deliberate answer made by withholding light. Hold its completed
outline before cutting.

Music: Remove pad and percussion for the entry. Isolated low and high notes
leave holes in the phrase. The absence must register without a borrowed crash.

### 20. A conversation

Bars 40-43; 2:21.818-2:36.364.

Zappy sends a short pulse. The lantern answers more slowly. The fold repeats
just enough of its edge-turn to identify itself; the occulter removes a
familiar patch of light. One speaks while the others hold. A phrase passes
between them with increasing certainty.

Within one held view, change observational bands to reveal different
filaments while registered landmarks stay in place. A spectral change
reveals structure, not just another colour.

Music: Return each character's rhythm in sequence. The four identities remain
distinct; the harmony begins connecting their answers. No continuous snare
roll.

### 21. Separate, faster

Bars 44-44; 2:36.364-2:40.000.

Beat 1: Zappy. Beat 2: lantern. Beat 3: fold. Beat 4: occulter. Four cuts,
each showing a gesture we already understand. This speed arrives only after
the earlier held views have taught the cast.

Music: Four distinct accents follow the cuts: low hit, bell, tom, short snare.
These are punctuation within the existing 66 BPM pulse.

### 22. Accumulating

Bars 45-45; 2:40.000-2:43.636.

Beat 1 returns to Zappy alone. Beat 2 adds the lantern to the composition.
Beat 3 adds the fold. Beat 4 adds the occulter. Keep every established panel
visible as the next appears; preserve their distinct orientations.

Music: Add one voice at a time: lead, pad, bass, then a brief percussion
pickup. The arrangement accumulates with the image.

### 23. All four

Bars 46-47; 2:43.636-2:50.909.

For exactly eight beats all four respondents move together. Each camera
follows the same normalised path around its own subject. Their familiar
gestures now agree in timing, while their scale and substance remain
different. Keep the composite intact for the entire passage.

Music: The four roles enter together at 46.1. A measured two-bar pulse
supports coordination; no frantic fill that prevents us seeing it.

### 24. The conversation stops

Bars 48-48; 2:50.909-2:54.545.

The composite holds. Motion quiets across all four panels. Wait a full bar.
Only then leave them for our instruments in space.

Music: All roles rest for a bar. Preserve decay; the next movement comes out
of that withdrawal.

### 25. The instruments above us

Bars 49-52; 2:54.545-3:09.091.

Hubble turns first, an edge of its door and body catching light. Webb follows;
its sunshield makes the changing orientation legible. Spitzer completes a
different slew. In bar 52 cut once to the quiet ground arrays, then back to
the last spacecraft settling. The instruments themselves turn; the camera does
not perform the movement for them.

Music: Three restrained low onsets at bars 49, 50 and 51. A small lead
fragment connects them. Bar 52 thins and leaves its last two beats without new
notes.

### 26. Leaving our position

Bars 53-54; 3:09.091-3:16.364.

Retreat from our location through nearby catalogue stars. Keep a small home
marker attached to one position until it becomes too small to see. Nearby
stars separate in depth; familiar sky directions change because we are moving
through the sample.

Music: A low, spacious version of the opening figure begins. Long tones and
sparse ticks give the pullback continuity without making it triumphant.

### 27. The galaxy

Bars 55-56; 3:16.364-3:23.636.

The nearby field becomes a small region of the Milky Way. The galaxy acquires
a full shape, then begins to diminish. Keep the location we came from legible
through the handoff. Detail remains in the dark lanes and uneven light.

Music: Widen the pad and sustain the bass. The lead rises only slightly;
apparent scale comes from space in the arrangement.

### 28. Neighbours

Bars 57-58; 3:23.636-3:30.909.

The Local Group becomes readable, then galaxies become points in a wider
distribution. Directions and positions follow retained catalogues where
supplied. The travel is compressed; the geography should not reorganise itself
to fit a pretty transition.

Music: The phrase grows denser but does not crash. A short descending figure
leads into the wider field.

### 29. Laniakea

Bars 59-60; 3:30.909-3:38.182.

The much larger structure resolves: irregular filaments, dark lanes, dense
knots and dim distant points, with no tidy radial spokes. Establish the galaxy
field before introducing any flow display. Uneven light travels through it as
the photography eases to a stop.

Music: A sustained, unsettled interval replaces the travelling pulse. End the
last new phrase early enough to hear it decay before the next hold.

### 30. Something in the pattern

Bars 61-62; 3:38.182-3:45.455.

Hold the wide field. At first its tensions and faint packets seem to be more
structure. A dark centre becomes legible through asymmetry and depth. In bar
62 nearly everything waits. No camera retreat is left to explain the next
change.

Music: Bar 61 releases its last low note after two beats. Bar 62 contains no
new notes. The held field has room to become threatening.

### 31. The same gesture

Bars 63-66; 3:45.455-4:00.000.

Bar 63: a strand turns edge-on. Bar 64: the dark opening widens as its frame
contracts. Bar 65: the same three-junction relationship crosses and returns,
now spread across an impossible scale. At 66.1 the movement completes and the
field visible through the opening changes. Landmarks outside it remain fixed.
Hold the camera through the impact and its aftermath.

Music: Repeat the small fold's exact four-bar rhythmic shape two octaves
lower. A delayed echo makes the resemblance audible. The largest low crash
lands at 66.1, as the geometry completes. Then stop adding attacks; let the
impact ring.

### 32. An answer comes back

Bars 67-68; 4:00.000-4:07.273.

A pulse leaves the changed structure along the route toward the place we came
from. It is small beside the reveal and unmistakably directed. Keep the
immense field in view while it travels. The movement has a consequence, and
the destination is us.

Music: A quieter low pulse at 67.1 follows the crash. A restrained answering
fragment travels above it. Bar 68 leaves a quiet unresolved pedal beneath the
consequence.

### 33. Heard

Bars 69-72; 4:07.273-4:21.818 plus release tail.

One brief return to Earth: the Kecks still hold together above the cloud sea.
Nothing here has yet changed. Return to the enormous opening and stay there
through the final bars and the complete musical tail. Let activity diminish
without undoing the fold. End in darkness after the consequence has had time
to land.

Music: No new attacks in bars 69-72; the quiet F-sharp/G pedal ties through
them. Do not resolve the final tension into a major cadence. Keep the full
generated release tail in the edit.

## Direction carried through every shot

Five principal instruments, four families: Keck I and II, Subaru, Rubin,
and ELT. Both Kecks share a pointing track and appear together. Their
foundations, floors and service yards remain fixed. Moving optics and
following enclosure openings have separate motions. Griffith is the
supporting city observer added to the screenplay.

Hawaii begins with Cloudsea: terrain, dense lower-slope trees, rocks,
equipment and warm layered mist. Foreground silhouettes stay almost black.
Warm interior light reveals the mirrors. Chile has specific ridges, roads
and service structures. Preserve the selected Glass Hour spooky reference.

Front, rear, interior and sky views have different jobs. An interior keeps
the enclosure present and looks through its aperture. Sharp beams begin
at attached launchers inside, follow their optics and remain occluded by
structure. Rubin is not automatically given ELT's four-beam equipment.

Searching means different targets, changes of elevation, settling and
abandonment. Each continuous shot has one coherent astronomical clock for
Sun, Moon, stars, Milky Way, illumination and shadows. Cuts can advance
time; the Moon never races through a repeated loop. Weather has its own
clock. Sunset and sunrise are visible events in the frame.

Use zoom sparingly. Downbeats offer a vocabulary of decisions, not an
obligation to flash, cut or move on every one. Significant moments hold.
The every-beat cuts and accumulating composite occur only in shots 21-23,
after the audience has learned the four respondents. Eight shared beats
mean the full two bars, without a cut away halfway through.

Keep the same identifiable junctions and four-stage operation in the small
fold and the enormous one. The return pulse is directed toward us. Hold
the consequence; brightness or a camera move must not hide the event.

## Sources and authored choices

The opening spectrogram is analysis of the resulting soundtrack, styled
as fictional instrumentation. It is not astronomical reception. Retain its
window, hop, frequency scale and sample alignment with the finished mix.

Use catalogue positions and directions where data are available, retaining
credits and coordinate frames. Gaia is only the local-star stage; the
Tully paper alone is not a measured galaxy catalogue or velocity field.
Travel compression and enlarged visibility are authored. The wider data
bridge remains work to do; do not silently substitute measured-looking
fiction. These structures are not all nested gravitationally bound shells.

The Laniakea visual language draws on the selected Sgr A* multiband imagery
and the sunspot reference: tangled filaments, irregular light and a dark
centre. Solar convection is not a physical model of the supercluster.
Flow visualisations and fictional folding geometry have distinct provenance.
The four respondents and their conversation are science fiction.
