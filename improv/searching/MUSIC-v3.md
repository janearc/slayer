## The new arrangement

Bars 1-4 keep the recognisable Glass Hour opening, with a soft low accent
for the Keck pair. Retain its F-sharp/G friction and delayed lead. The
night-pad lead, stereo pads, bass, reverb and delay settings stay intact.

Bars 5-14 become a varied, spare introduction: Rubin's small metal tick,
longer interior tones, ELT's low punctuation, a rear-sky questioning phrase,
the sparse Griffith view, descending sunset and an unresolved dawn.
The old early snare wash is removed.

Bars 15-16 build. Bar 17 ends its new notes after two beats, then bars
18-19 rest in every role. This drains into the shared lock. Reverb and
delay continue: a score rest does not mean digital silence in the mix.

Bars 20 and 22 begin the two arrays with low hits. Bar 23 again stops
new notes after two beats. At 24.1 a kick and metallic wash introduce the
orbital, with the lead entering a beat later. Zappy's shorter phrase
follows; bar 27 clears room before the lantern.

Bars 28-31 use long pad notes, a quiet bell and a patient lead. Bars 32-35
teach a new fold motif: C-sharp, F-sharp, G/F-sharp/C-sharp, low F-sharp.
Its onset/rest pattern follows the four stages of the visible gesture.

Bars 36-39 make the occulter a withdrawal, with isolated notes rather than
another loud introduction. Bars 40-43 revisit the four voices separately.
Bar 44 gives the four individual cuts distinct percussion accents.

At bar 45 the lead is present first, the pad enters on beat 2, bass on 3
and percussion on 4. Bars 46-47 bring all four roles together for exactly
eight beats. Bar 48 withdraws all new notes before the spacecraft turn.

Bars 49-51 have three restrained onsets; bar 52 clears its last two beats.
Bars 53-60 extend the Glass Hour motif across the catalogue pullback with
long pad and bass notes. The last phrase ends early. Bar 61 releases a
low note after two beats; bar 62 is a complete score rest.

Bars 63-66 repeat the small fold's lead rhythm and pitch shape exactly,
two octaves lower, with heavier supporting harmony. The largest impact
is at 66.1, 236.363636 seconds, when the geometry completes. A quieter
directed pulse follows at 67.1, 240 seconds.

Bar 68 leaves a very quiet unresolved F-sharp/G pedal under the consequence.
Those notes tie through bars 69-72 without new attacks. Let the entire
release tail finish; no final triumphant cadence and no abrupt audio trim.

## Impacts and instruments

The separate voicing changes the original open-hat slot, o, into a longer
synthetic metallic wash: a struck plate plus filtered noise. The first
crash is (k o) at mf; the final is (k o b) at f. Only those two bars use o.
This is an authored crash-like sound, not a recorded cymbal sample.

The existing k, s, h, t and b sounds remain. Master is 0.40 rather than
0.43 to leave room for the additional impact energy. The complete score
must synthesize without clipping before delivery. Numerical checks can
confirm timing and energy changes; musical balance still needs listening.

## One clock

The exact time of bar B beat b is ((B - 1) * 4 + b - 1) * 60 / 66.
Compute every boundary from this absolute position. The first four-fps
picture at or after a cue is ceil(time * 4); never accumulate rounded
beat lengths. For finer beat cuts, sample each frame's original score time.

The music verb writes the rendered WAV, exact composition and voicing,
source hashes, sample count and a grid with all 288 authored beats.
Its hollow marks count through rests; they are not detected attacks.
Any revised notes or voicing get a new WAV and hashes, even at the same BPM.

EDIT-v3.txt and the script share the same boundaries. A browser review
should follow the audio element's currentTime for active scene, bar and
beat. Seeking either must seek the same audio. Keep script changes
separate from claims that a scene has been rendered.

The opening spectrogram will be measured from this resulting mix after
the arrangement is settled. The previous soundtrack's analysis cannot
be reused. Save window, hop, frequency axis and original sample offsets.
