# heard.

A music film for b-m-a-s. Observatories search through passing days.
Something answers. Other things answer it. Instruments on Earth and in
space turn together, and a familiar gesture reveals an impossible scale.

This is the revised screenplay. The existing 320-pixel, six-fps movie
proves the earlier search, lock and blazar sequence. It does not yet
depict the cast or final reveal described here. Its saved inputs remain
the record of what was actually rendered.

## Opening title

Black screen. heard. Small, lowercase, with the period. Hold before the
first observatory emerges. The final response changes what the word meant.

## The musical rule

One: a decision. Start a slew, move the camera, reveal an object, send
a pulse, or begin a fold. Choose a strong gesture the audience can read.

use the zoom effect sparingly. create a library of "one" effects, do not use them on every 1-beat. for significant moments, linger. don't shift away. then, you even want to shift on every beat:

```
moment - hold
 2
 3
 4
1 - subtle, but move
 2
 3
 4
1 - cast member 2
2 - cast member 3
3 - cast member 4
1 - back to 1
2 - composite [ 1 and 2 ]
3 - composite [ 1 and 2 and 3 ]
4 - composite [ 1 and 2 and 3 and 4 ]
1 - composite, all move together for 8 beats, camera motion follows the same path for each.
```

Two: continue with less energy. A specifically cued cymbal may interrupt;
do not invent a cymbal detection or flash on every second beat.

Three: normally no new action. Carry the current movement more slowly. Give the
eye time to follow the structure, weather or travelling light.

Four: nearly hold. A light recedes, motion almost stops, or saturation
drains slightly. Leave room for the next one.

Camera movement integrates these changes continuously. A deliberate cut
is separate from a camera move. Held moments still contain actual motion
and newly rendered frames. The music stays at its original playback speed.


## The cast

The ground observers are the first characters. Hawaii and the fictional
Atacama site have distinct silhouettes, terrain and weather. Subaru joins
the Hawaiian ensemble; Rubin joins the Chilean ensemble. These are authored
sites using observatory-inspired recipes, not reconstructions of real sites.
add griffith park. using griffith park lets us have a low hazy view of the observatory from the haze of los angeles.

Four respondents each have a gesture we can learn:

- Orbital and Zappy form the first scene: machinery, sharp electrical
  activity and a pulse that travels somewhere we can follow.
- The lantern answers with a warmer, slower swell across a phrase.
  Its changes feel patient beside Zappy's urgency.
- The folding object answers through changing geometry. Persistent
  junctions and a travelling light let us follow one precise operation.
- The occulter is a working fourth character: it answers by crossing
  a luminous field and exposing a shaped absence. Its final design is
  still open; it must have a voice distinct from another glowing object.

The megastructure is the fifth presence and the final reveal. It shares
the folding object's gesture. We do not see enough of it to recognize
that relationship until the end.

## Ground light and space texture

The selected ground reference is the spooky summit low view of
12 November. Keep near-black foreground trees and enclosure surfaces,
warm mist separating distant silhouettes, and amber guide beams carrying
the brightest light. Avoid a broad blue fill that flattens the scene.

Its lighting arrangement uses a low Moon and layered atmosphere. Carry
the arrangement and visual relationships into the new sets; exposure
numbers belong to their rendering path and need their own matching.

The selected space reference is Sgr A* in Four Lights from 2 November.
Look for tangled filaments, asymmetric clouds, dense points of light,
dark lanes and a few intensely bright regions. Leave detail to discover
away from the brightest point. Smooth rings and featureless glowing lobes
do not meet this reference.

Registered observational layers can be the instruments' views. Changing
bands should reveal different structures with consistent framing. The
fictional respondents inhabit authored scenes; the observational plates
do not acquire invented three-dimensional depth.

## Prologue: two listeners

Begin low near the Hawaiian instruments. A dome edge and trees are
barely visible against warm distant mist. A guide-beam gesture establishes
attention. Show enough of the instrument for its movement to be legible.

Cut to the Chilean site: bare ground, a different enclosure and thinner
weather. Establish both places before the search begins to alternate.
Use Subaru and Rubin as recognizable members of the ensemble.

Keep the yard equipment and ground fixed. An instrument slews inside its
enclosure; the entire building must not rotate as a substitute for a mount.
The enclosure opening follows where appropriate.

Guide beams originate at launchers attached to the instrument assembly
inside the enclosure. They follow the instrument and pass through its
opening. The shell occludes the beam where it should; do not start the
light at the shell just to avoid obstruction.

Use the owner's shared-pointing YAML as the reference for beam alignment,
small guide-star offsets and authored display controls. Its ELT placement
coordinates are specific to that instrument. Adapt attachment positions
for each assembly. Different observers need not all carry four lasers.

## Search: days pass

The sites search independently. On one, a telescope makes a decision;
through two and three its movement quiets; on four it almost rests.

Alternate on musical phrases, with occasional wider shots showing the
other instruments. Mist moves through the Hawaiian trees and more sparsely
over the Chilean ground. Sun and Moon rise and set rapidly. We should see
time passing through changing skies and shadows, not just an exposure fade.

Dawn is a brief rim of colour. Night returns to the chosen summit lighting.
The weather and astronomical scenery have an accelerated scene clock;
the song and its cues do not accelerate with them.

Later, let one site seem almost to finish a movement the other began.
They repeat an aim, hesitate and abandon it. Their eventual stillness
needs this restlessness first.

## The swell: shared lock

The owner identified the swell at bar 48 beat 4, approximately 2:03.
The viewed grid places it at 123.01 seconds. Its sound may be a voice;
do not call it strings. A possible local timing change remains to check.

Settle the search just before this cue. The Hawaiian instrument stops.
Cut to Chile: its instrument stops too. Their beam directions agree in
the authored story. Hold long enough to recognize the absence of searching.

Let the swell carry a very slow camera approach. Suspend the repeated
beat-one punches. Mist keeps flowing, but the rapid passage of days
subsides into a held night.

They have found something. We have not seen it.

## The radio response

On a downbeat, an entire compact dish field begins turning together.
Finish the move over the phrase and hold. Show the field wide enough
to prove coordinated motion across separate antennas.

A second, more widely spread field answers with the same aim. Give it
a different silhouette and camera position. Fixed piers remain still;
only the articulated assemblies move.

Return briefly to a steady optical beam. The chain of attention now
extends across the ground observers. No light-speed or geographic
pointing solution is claimed for this musical compression.

## First answer: orbital and Zappy

On the returning downbeat, leave Earth. Reveal enough of the orbital
structure to understand its scale and orientation before adding activity.

Zappy wakes. An electrical pulse travels across a readable piece of
machinery. Follow its path, then let it recede through the remaining
beats. Darkness leaves the surrounding structure partly unresolved.

This is a substantial reveal, but it is the first answer. Do not spend
the final wide view or the largest scale here.

## Other voices

The lantern answers Zappy with a warm swell. Hold its shape long enough
to learn the gesture before returning to the orbital scene.

The folding object enters in a different phrase. A dark rib turns
edge-on and becomes almost invisible. Behind it, a much larger surface
appears where there seemed to be no room for it.

An opening widens while its frame contracts. Junctions pass behind one
another and emerge in an arrangement we struggle to reconcile. Keep
recognizable landmarks throughout: this is one deliberate operation,
not an unrelated random shape on each beat.

The fourth respondent answers with an occlusion. Something luminous
disappears in a precise shape, then returns along a different edge.
Let the absence carry the gesture.

## Conversation

Return to each respondent in a phrase we can recognize. Their gestures
begin independently, then agree in timing. A Zappy pulse precedes a
lantern swell; the fold follows; the shaped absence answers.

Different spectral views can expose different parts of a respondent or
the field around it. Keep fixed landmarks across those changes. Retained
observational layers and authored component views have separate provenance.

Begin with separate gestures. Later, build the owner's accumulating
composite and let all four move together for eight beats with matching
camera paths. Before that coordinated passage, do not move everything at once. One acts while the others give
us something stable to watch. On four, allow an anticipation that the
next one can resolve.

## The instruments in space

Before the final reveal, space observatories also turn toward the shared
direction. Use the available Hubble, Webb and Spitzer-inspired assemblies
as candidates, with their individual shot assignments still to be chosen.

First, make one spacecraft's slew unmistakable. Let a sunshield or a
structural edge catch enough light to explain its changing orientation.
Then show another instrument settling on the same aim.

The movement must belong to the spacecraft or its actual moving assembly,
not to a camera orbit that leaves the instrument unchanged. Their different
bodies should not perform identical copied animations.

Cut once to Earth. The dishes hold. The optical beams are steady.
Return to space, where the last instrument finishes turning. Give us
a brief wait before the thing they are attending to becomes legible.

## The wide field: Laniakea

Establish a field inspired by the Laniakea reconstruction. Galaxies become
points in a much larger arrangement. Sweeping flows provide a pattern
the eye can begin to follow.

The familiar Laniakea strands depict reconstructed motions, not glowing
physical cables. Preserve that meaning in any observational presentation.
The transition into the fictional scene is an authored visual association.

Hold the camera nearly still. Persistent galaxy landmarks let us tell
that a change belongs to the object rather than to our viewpoint.

## Final reveal: the opening

One faint strand moves across the established pattern. Another follows.
At first we could mistake them for more of the field's complexity.

A dark opening begins to form. Junctions change their relation around it.
We recognize the exact folding gesture the small respondent performed,
now taking place at an impossible scale.

A moving edge reveals another edge behind it. What appeared to be a
complete object becomes a small part of something much larger. The four
respondents can appear as tiny reference points within the authored
composition; the montage does not assert measured cosmic positions.

On the chosen downbeat, a coordinated wave reaches the junctions. The
structure completes one deliberate movement. The starfield visible
through the opening changes while the field outside it stays fixed.

Hold the camera. Let the audience see what happened. Do not hide the
moment with a cut, a flare covering the frame, or an immediate zoom.
The reveal needs time to land after the musical impact.

## Coda

Return briefly to the quiet observers if the final phrase supports it.
Their attention now has a subject we understand differently.

End with the opening and enough unmoving surroundings to retain scale.
Light or activity can recede; do not undo the movement or explain the
structure's purpose. Leave a final newly rendered hold.
