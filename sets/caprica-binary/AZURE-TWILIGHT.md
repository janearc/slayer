# Azure: high flock in twilight

The direction is a high-altitude flock catching low light over dark,
brooding clouds, with two distant suns. On Earth, high noctilucent clouds
can remain illuminated by sunlight when it is already below a lower
observer's horizon; NASA describes their high ice clouds reflecting sunlight
in [Polar Mesospheric Clouds](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/polar-mesospheric-clouds-south-pacific-ocean-80217/)
and [Nocturnal Wonders](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/nocturnal-wonders/).
The visual direction borrows the brightness contrast, not an Earth altitude,
cloud composition or physical model of Azure. Pterosaurs on Azure, their
flight and survival, the dark blue sky, lower cloud geometry and colours are
explicit artistic choices.

This unrendered composition keeps the twelve V11 pose placements and the
translated binary. The separate nearby sun-disc cues remain deliberately
enlarged and elevated away from the actual stellar directions; they are not
the suns' true positions or sizes. A made-up low directional light and weak
fill aim to make the animals' surfaces reflect cool light against a dark
layer, not self-emit. This lighting does not reproduce occlusion by the
planet or derive its intensity from the binary. The lower `cloud-decks`
field is a prescribed 3-D noise volume, not simulated weather, and can
extend beyond the nominal giant gas boundary. Azure's generated gas medium
is retained with an artistic low opacity of 0.4 and the same depth; prior
controls show opacity 4 only modestly reduces the pale horizon, so the
appearance at 0.4 is unverified. The generic GPU grid and single-scattering
renderer limit cloud detail and illumination.

The source photography is evidence of Earth's lighting geometry only. The
scene does not claim actual noctilucent cloud particles on Azure or a
physically coherent two-sun eclipse. Framing, twelve separate readable
animals, the cloud bank, color and both disc cues must be judged in a render.

Job 594 showed a dark lower bank but the animals read as nearly black
silhouettes; the pale horizon and horizontal bands remained. The artistic
key was aimed almost horizontally toward the primary, approximately the
camera's viewing direction, so it tends toward backlighting and offers
horizontal surfaces little direct light; the image alone does not isolate
normals, occlusion or attenuation as causes. V13 adds a moderate cool
camera-side grazing light, a weaker warm edge light, and slightly stronger
fill, and matches artistic sky ground/horizon colours. These are extra
photographic lights, not new modeled stars or luminous animals. Their effect
on the cloud bank, horizon and the twelve silhouettes remains unverified.

The horizon and cloud perspective were judged wrong in job 602.
V14 moves the camera to 25 m, about 5 m inside the nominal gas boundary,
and tips it down across the flock. It replaces the near 230 m floating
cloud box with an invented feathered bank 14 km wide, with its top 700 m
below the local datum and its near edge 500 m away. This is an artistic
flat local layer, not simulated Azure weather; its extent and position are
chosen for perspective. The generated giant is retained at an explicitly
artistic opacity of 0.0004, so the gas may become visually negligible.
Azure's approximately 46,726 km radius makes curvature very subtle over
kilometre distances; no exaggerated curved horizon is asserted. A roughly
146 m GPU grid cell in the deck's broadest dimension may blur features.
The camera change, cloud appearance, silhouette separation and two cues
are not yet visually verified.

Job 608 showed a broader, dark cloud layer below the flock, though it was
soft and the animals remained brown; the two cue discs touched the upper
edge. V15 changes their reflective animal appearance to the explicitly
artistic `detailed-silver` study and weakens the warm artistic edge light.
It moves the separate, invented cue discs lower in the frame without changing
the true modeled binary. The pale hide and plain membranes are not an
evidence-based reconstruction or self-emission; posed membrane paint is
still omitted on GPU. Silvery readability, full bodies and both cues inside
frame remain unverified until rendered.

V15 was inspected as twelve separated forms and two cues, but small animals
look broken or stippled; the cloud bank remained blurred. The model builds
3.5 mm-wide body fibre blades and 12 mm-wide membrane ridges. At 19 m, a
960-pixel frame sees roughly 2.5 cm per pixel, so such features are often
subpixel; the distant animals are smaller still. This makes sampling a
plausible contributor, not an established sole cause. The separate
`v15-sampled` job keeps the exact scene/camera/film size but uses four rays
per side (16 samples per pixel) instead of one, for one targeted visual
comparison. It is not an animation or temporal-stability assessment.

The independent observer found the 16-sample still's lead body and wings
visibly smoother and several distant forms more continuous than V15's
one-sample image. Regular wing lines and some gaps remain, while the blurred
cloud bank is substantially unchanged. This is one still comparison and
does not establish temporal stability.
